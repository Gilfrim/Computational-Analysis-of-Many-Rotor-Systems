\begin{block}{Premise}

We use the time-independent Schrödinger Equation to extract eigenvalues and eigenvectors, with hamiltonian:
\begin{equation}
\hat{H} = B \hat{K} + g\hat{V}, \  B,g: \text{adjustable parameters}
\end{equation}
Where the hamiltonian describes a coplanar rotor system.

We solve Schrödinger equation by diagonalizing the hamiltonian over the product basis of single site plane waves:
\begin{align}
    \hat{H}\ket{\psi} &= E\ket{\psi}
\end{align}

However, this hamiltonian become extremely large really fast which makes exact diagonalization computationally heavy.

\textit{(Even though we are currently working with systems of rotors the method is equal applicable to magnetic systems.)}

\begin{alertblock}{Problem Statement}
Generalize Coupled Cluster methods (CC) to rotor systems, to explore larger systems. Compare with other approaches and also consider Thermal Properties.
\end{alertblock}

\end{block}

\begin{block}{Improving Exact Diagonalization}
In order to explore larger systems with Exact Diagonalization and Configuration Coupled Cluster (CCC) we use Natural Orbitals:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Truncate the basis using one particle reduced density matrix for small system (2-3 sites), and use selected eigenvectors (Natural Orbital, NO).
  \item Use sparse matrix techniques to construct Hamiltonian and use direct diagonalization.
  \item Systems up to about 9 sites with 5 NO's can be studied.
\end{itemize}

\begin{block}{Configurational Coupled Cluster}
    We define our $\hat{T}_{1}$ and $\hat{T}_{2}$ operators in terms of single-site excitation operators $\hat{e}^{a}_{i}$ where $i$ indicates a single low-lying state:
    \begin{align}
        \hat{T}_1&= \sum_{x} \sum_{a,i} t^{a}_{i}(x) \hat{e}^{a}_{i}(x)\\
        \hat{T}_2&= \sum_{x,y} \sum_{a,b,i,j} t^{ab}_{ij}(x, y) \hat{e}^{a}_{i}(x) \hat{e}^{b}_{j}(y)
    \end{align}
    The $\hat{T}_{1}$ and $\hat{T}_{2}$ operators form the overall $\hat{T}$ in the CC ansatz:
    \begin{align}
        \ket{\psi} = e^{\hat{T}}\ket{0}
    \end{align}
    Substituting this form into the Schrödinger Equation and projecting against the basis set $\ket{\tau}$, the equations for the cluster amplitudes can be expressed as:
    \begin{align}
        \bra{\tau} e^{-\hat{T}} \hat{H} e^{\hat{T}} \ket{0} = E \delta_{\tau 0}
    \end{align}

    By expanding one can arrive at a full set of algebraic equations which are solved numerically through iterative and/or imaginary time propagation procedures.\newline

    \textbf{Features of CCC:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item method is size extensive
        \item the number of parameters scales like $nstate^2 * nsite$ for periodic systems
        \item method is only accurate up to $g \approx 0.4$
    \end{itemize}

\end{block}
